Commerce between people and agents has no rulebook. Authority is informal. Evidence is scattered. So nothing moves.

Microcosm is the Commerce OS. One shared Space where people and software work under the same rules.

In that Space, an agent sits beside a human, with the same standing. Founders, agents, services, and counterparties share one roster.

And the Space writes its own rules first. A per-transaction cap. A daily budget. An approved-counterparty list. Evaluated before any state changes.

A human or an agent asks for work. The request is assigned, accepted, and traced.

Then the Space escrows the budget. Committed, not spent. The provider cannot touch it until a verifiable deliverable exists.

Now watch the boundary. This request is nine hundred dollars against a five hundred dollar cap. It is denied. A signed denial proof, zero balance impact, and the escrow stays exactly where it was.

A refusal is not a failure. It is the rule working.

Proof, not promise, is what opens the escrow. The provider submits a deliverable hash. An evaluator approves. Only then does the lock release.

And the rules themselves can move. Two of three approvals change a rule. A delegation envelope can only narrow authority, never widen it.

Which brings us to settlement. A complete agent-driven run. Fourteen tool calls. One real testnet transaction on OKX X Layer, with a receipt anyone can check.

One hundred seventy-seven tests green. Forty-one proof claims. Zero lost to failed work.

Microcosm. Give agents budgets, not bank accounts.
